What it does

The chart

Lagna chart in North Indian, South Indian and chakra styles, with every graha's sign, degree, house, nakshatra, pada, dignity, retrogression and combustion.

Sixteen vargas

D1 to D60 by the Parashari rules, including navamsa for marriage and dasamsa for work.

Dashas

Vimshottari to three levels with the balance at birth, and the Yogini cycle alongside it.

Shadbala and ashtakavarga

All six strengths in rupas against each graha's own minimum, every bhinnashtakavarga, and the sarvashtakavarga that always totals 337.

Gochar

Transits counted from your natal Moon, with the bindus of each sign being crossed, Sade Sati phase dates, and the next Jupiter and Saturn returns.

Muhurta finder

Pick the work — griha pravesh, a vehicle, a journey, a new business — and every choghadiya slot across the next weeks is scored, with the reasons shown.

Hastrekha

Trace your own palm lines over an outline, or over a photo of your hand. No camera pretends to understand a hand: you draw, and the classical rules read what you drew, naming the measurement behind every sentence.

Ask by voice

Speak the question in Hindi or English and hear the answer read back, with the chart factors it came from listed underneath.

Festivals and vrat

A computed calendar for any year and any town. Each date comes from the tithi running at the moment the rule asks for — sunrise, midday, dusk or midnight — with Bhadra moving Holika Dahan the way a panchang does.

Varshphal

The solar return to the arc-minute, Muntha, and the lord of the year picked from the five contenders by strength.

Panchang

Tithi, nakshatra, yoga, karana and vara with end times, sunrise and sunset, Rahu Kaal, Abhijit, choghadiya and horas, for any date and any town.

Milan and namkaran

Ashtakoota to 36 gunas with every koota explained, and the syllable a newborn's name should start with, from the pada of their nakshatra.

Prashna and Ank Jyotish

A chart for the moment a question is asked, and the number reading — mulank, bhagyank, namank and the Lo Shu grid — that people ask for next.

Upay

Remedies chosen from your chart, not handed to everyone alike: mantra and its japa count, fasting day, daan, and one thing to do today that costs nothing.

The 27 nakshatras, 12 rashis and 9 grahas on one page each, in English and Hindi: lords, deities, padas, name syllables, dignities and dasha years, written from the same engine the app computes with.

Today's panchang · आज का पंचांग

Tithi, nakshatra, yoga, karana and vara with end times, Rahu Kaal, Abhijit, choghadiya, the next seven days and the festivals coming up, for Delhi. Rebuilt every morning.

Drawn, not downloaded

A swamiji sits on the Ask screen, drawn entirely in code. He breathes, blinks, tilts his head while the microphone is open, closes his eyes while the chart is read, and moves his mouth to the voice when the answer is spoken. No animation file, no likeness of anyone, a few kilobytes.

Waiting is spent on the 27 nakshatras: the loader draws each mansion's own symbol star by star — Ashwini's horse head, Rohini's cart, Revati's fish — names it and its deity, and walks on to the next while the work finishes.

Computed here, not fetched

The astrology runs inside the app in plain Dart. Planetary positions come from our own series, fitted to NASA JPL's public-domain DE440s kernel, and held to this in continuous integration:

BodyWorst longitude error, 1900–2070
Sununder 2″
Moonunder 8″
Mercury, Venus, Jupiter, Saturnunder 3″
Marsunder 4″

Independently, the Sun lands within 0.4″ of 0° at six March equinoxes between 1905 and 2051, and Gandhi's published chart reproduces sign for sign.

Your details stay yours

Birth details are the most identifying data a person has. They are stored in this device's own storage and nowhere else. There is no account, no sign-in, no analytics on anything you type, and the app works with the network off.

What it will not do

No predictions about death, illness, pregnancy, exams, court cases or investments. No fear-based framing of doshas. No verdict on whether a marriage should happen. Readings are presented as what the tradition says, with the classical source named.
